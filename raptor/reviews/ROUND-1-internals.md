# Round 1: internals (channels, manifolds, injector, powerhead cavities)

Method: radial scans of the implicit fields (`cooled = tca - channels - ports`, rings, powerhead) every 40 mm in z, plus sections `int_y0`, `int_z1555`, `int_z1992`, zooms `int_throat`, `int_lcring`, `int_head`, `int_powerhead` (in /private/tmp/claude-501/review/).

## Findings

- **INT-1 major: the ports cut through the hot wall.** In `manifold_ports` each capsule (r 4) starts at `r_gas + 2`, so its round end reaches `r_gas - 2`. That makes 108 Ø8 holes into the gas path at z 1152, 1462 and 1826. A radial scan at y=0 through those heights finds no metal between the gas side and the ring. Fix: keep each port at n ≥ 1 + depth (start at `r_gas + 1 + depth + 4`) and reach the channels through a collector groove (INT-2).
- **INT-2 major: 252 of the 360 chamber channels connect to no manifold.** All 36 ports sit at the same angles on every ring. They touch 108 channels at 1462 and 1826 and only 36 at 1152. The other channels have no inlet or outlet, so no coolant flows in them. Fix: cut a full-circumference collector groove at each ring station (about 8 mm along z, n from 1 to about 5) so it crosses every channel, then run the radial ports out from the groove.
- **INT-3 major: both channel runs have a dead end.** As coded, coolant enters at 1462 and runs up to 1826 and down to 1152. The upper channels go on from 1826 to 1979, the hottest 150 mm next to the injector, with no outlet. The 720 bell channels run from 1152 down to z 40 with no lip manifold and no return. Fix one of two ways: (a) add a head-end collector under the disc at about 1975 and a turnaround at the lip (interleaved return channels, or a small lip torus inside the rolled lip), or (b) end the channels at the ring stations and change the circuit description to match.
- **INT-4 major: the lower chamber ring does not touch the chamber.** At z 1462 the skin is at r 207.4 and the inside of the ring tube is at 212, leaving a 4.6 mm gap. The 36 ports open into air there; `int_lcring` shows daylight in the gap. Fix: R 241 with a 39 mm tube keeps the 0.56 m OD and closes the gap. A saddle or web works too.
- **INT-5 major: the base disc fills the top of the combustion chamber.** The disc is solid from z 1933 to 2017 across r < 170, and the bay is cut only from 1984 up. So the top 51 mm of the chamber is metal, and the injector face looks onto a solid plug (`int_powerhead`). The channels from 1933 to 1979 also lie inside the disc. Fix: subtract the gas-side volume from the disc, or start the bay at 1930.
- **INT-6 major: 69 elements get no oxygen.** The outer element ring (r 154.5) lies outside the ox dome (r 145). Its post tops and bores (z 2035–2047) end inside the solid valve body. Fix: widen the dome base to r ≥ 162, or move the ring to r ≤ 141.
- **INT-7 minor: the injector plates are not attached to anything.** The face and back plates are r 170 inside a bay of r 171, and the posts start 1 mm above the face plate and stand clear of its holes. Nothing holds the plates, and a 1 mm ring slot connects the fuel cavity straight to the chamber. Fix: make the bay r 170 and braze the rim, or make the slot a deliberate film-cooling gap.
- **INT-8 minor: the fuel slots break through the top of the disc.** The 16 slots span z 2002–2018 and the disc top is at 2017, so they open 1 mm to the outside at r 172–225, around the base of the valve body. Fix: centre the slots at 2008 or make them 14 mm tall.
- **INT-9 minor: the coolant flow areas do not match.** The duct bore is Ø130 (13,300 mm²), the lower-ring cavity is Ø58 (2,640 mm²), the ports total 1,810 mm², and the channels at 1462 total about 1,500 mm². The ring is the choke. The round end of the duct bore (r 65) also cuts the ring tube (r 34) right through. A larger ring needs more OD than the measured value allows, so consider a D-section ring.
- **INT-10 note: the throat ring is a closed hollow.** It has a cavity but no ports. Either make it solid or give it film-cooling injection (UNKNOWN §4).
- **INT-11 note: the ox-rich dome has no inlet and a thin wall.** Its top is at 2230 and the solid turbopump housing starts at 2236, so nothing feeds it. The wall to the valve body is 13 mm: pr/t ≈ 45 MPa·145/13 ≈ 500 MPa at about 800 K, which is high. Use 20 mm or more and open the dome to the turbine exhaust.
- **INT-12 note: these parts are good.** Throat channels are 0.96 × 2.2 mm with a 1.0 mm hot wall and 1.0 mm lands (`int_z1555`). No channel breaks the outer skin at any scanned station. The bell closeout is 5.6 mm at z 60, rising to 13–18 mm higher up; the gas loads are low there, so this is adequate. The bell has no ribs. The chamber is one solid 50–80 mm thick, with no separate liner and jacket; that is acceptable for a printed part but heavy. The injector has 430 elements on rings 13.5 mm apart radially and 14 mm around (UNKNOWN §4 range), with 1.7 mm fuel annuli and the outer ring 15 mm from the wall.
- **INT-13 note: the section renders contain artefacts.** The y:0 caps show dark triangles and a zigzag in the disc. The z:1992 caps fill the element holes outside the |y| < 6 band, and the throat-band channels show white see-through gaps. These are meshing or capping artefacts, not cavities.

**Verdict:** the channel geometry is plausible, but the coolant circuit has five major topology errors (INT-1 to INT-5) and the injector one more (INT-6). The internals are not yet physically consistent.
